Privacy

Last updated 25 September 2026

Tomato watches your screen so it can tell you when you have drifted off the thing you said you were going to do. That is an unusual amount of trust to ask for, so here is exactly what happens to that data — and what does not.

The short version. Screen recording and your session journal stay on your Mac. Short text summaries of your activity are sent to Anthropic so the app can describe what you are doing. Anonymous counts — how many sessions you ran, which setup steps you finished — are sent to PostHog. Your screen content never leaves your machine, and you can switch the anonymous counts off in Settings.

What stays on your Mac

Tomato uses a local screen recorder to capture what is on screen — window titles, browser tabs, and on-screen text. That capture, and the journal of focus sessions built from it, are written to a database on your own machine. They are never uploaded anywhere.

What goes to Anthropic

Every few minutes, Tomato sends a short text description of your recent activity to Anthropic's API, using your own API key, and gets back a one-line summary plus a judgement about whether you have drifted. No screenshots or video are sent — only brief text. Anthropic's handling of that text is governed by their own terms.

What goes to PostHog

Tomato sends a small set of anonymous counts to PostHog so we can tell whether people who download the app actually get through setup and come back. This is the complete list — there is nothing else:

  • app_launched — app version, and whether this was the first launch on this machine
  • onboarding_step_completed — which setup step finished: screen permission, accessibility permission, or API key
  • onboarding_completed — app version
  • session_started — the planned length of the session, in minutes
  • session_ended — planned length, actual length, whether it ended early, and why
  • hud_toggled — whether you expanded or collapsed the timer

On this website, PostHog also records page views and three events on the download button: that you opened the email form, that you dismissed it, and that you completed the download. Autocapture and session replay are switched off in both the app and the website — we do not record your clicks, your keystrokes, or your screen.

What is never collected

  • Screen content. No screenshots, no video, no captured on-screen text.
  • Window titles and browser tabs. Used locally, never transmitted to us.
  • Your goal text. What you type as your session intention stays on your machine.
  • Your Anthropic API key. Encrypted at rest on your Mac, and never included in any event.
  • Your name, email, or account. Tomato has no accounts and no login.

How you are identified

Analytics events are tagged with a one-way hash of your Mac's hardware identifier, truncated to 32 characters. It cannot be reversed back to your machine, and it is not linked to your email or to anything you type. Its only job is to let us tell one returning user from two new ones.

PostHog also derives an approximate country from the IP address the request arrives on. The IP address itself is not stored as an event property.

Turning analytics off

Open Settings from the Tomato menu-bar icon and switch off Share anonymous usage data. It takes effect immediately — no restart — and nothing further is sent. The app works exactly the same either way.

The email you give us

If you enter an email to download Tomato, it is stored by our email provider and used only to send occasional product updates. Every message has an unsubscribe link.
